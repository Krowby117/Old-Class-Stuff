## Learning Outcomes:

#### 1. Identify and justify at least one source of requirements in software engineering 

- A key source of requirements is users / customers.
  - Its important to know exactly what the customers want in their product as well as what users expect to be able to do.

#### 2. Discuss at least four practical strategies for gathering requirements in an agile software development setting 

***User Interviews*** Ch. 4 page 46
- Most used approach to trawling for stories.
- Allows you talk to all types of users and get big picture ideas.
- Best to ask open-ended, context-free questions for a wider range of answers.
  - "Is performance more important in some parts of the application?"
  - "Would you be willing to give up aspects of the software if it were to run in a browser?"

***Questionnaires*** Ch. 4 page 47-48
- Best used for gathering additional information about stories you already have.
  - Should not be used as a primary technique of trawling for new stories.
- Examples:
  - How often they use certain features.
  - Why they don't use other features more often.

***Observation*** Ch. 4 page 48
- Personal Experience:
  - When developing a game last year we had a live testing day where we watched players and took notes on things they struggled with and things they enjoyed.

***Story-Writing workshops*** Ch. 4 page 49
- Allows you to bounce ideas off of your team members and brain storm stories.
- A priority and judgement free zone can allow for interesting ideas to be brought up

#### 4. Explain the role of acceptance tests regarding project requirements
- They act as criteria to confirm if a user story has been correctly implemented.
- Acceptance tests are the customer’s responsibility to specify.


#### 5. Summarize at least three “best practices” that will likely improve the effectiveness of acceptance testing 
**Customer written**
- Customers specify tests with help from developers/testers to ensure business needs are captured

**Create tests before coding**
- Define tests before starting work to guide development and reduce ambiguity

**Tests need to be simple and clear**
- Write tests that can be objectively executed and verified
